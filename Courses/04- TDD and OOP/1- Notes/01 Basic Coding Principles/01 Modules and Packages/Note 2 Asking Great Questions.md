# What makes a good question?
What do you do when you get stuck on a coding problem? Always start with due diligence 
1. Read error messages and logs to isolate bugs 
2. Look up error messages 
3. Review relevant sections of reading 
4. Check official documentation 
5. Discuss with your peer 

Still stuck? Time for the **15 minute rule**.
## The 15 minute rule
The goal of the 15 minute rule is to come up with a great question in 15 minutes 

Recall these traits of a _good_ coding question:
1. It's specific 
2. It's clear and concise 
3. It shows that you've put work into it

To go from _good_ to _great_, the question must also have the following characteristics:
1. It can be answered by an instructor with little effort 
2. The answer unblocks you 

Step 1,2 and 3 are the key to achieving steps 4 and 5 

## Put work into the question 
The 15 minute rule involves 4 steps 
1. Write down everything you **do** understand about the problem 
2. Write down everything you **don't** understand about the problem 
3. Based on 1 and 2, write down a **specific** question 
4. Clean up the question by **eliminating** unnecessary words 

After due diligence (step 0), you will have a partial understanding of the problem. There may be gaps of confusion but identifying the parts you _do_ understand will help to isolate and identify the parts you don't.

Once you identify the most significant gaps of confusion, write down a question to address them as specifically as possible. Specific may mean focusing on just one major point of confusion or combining multiple points into one overarching conceptual question.

Finally, go back through your question and eliminate _all_ unnecessary words. Your question should be clear, concise, and straight to the point.
